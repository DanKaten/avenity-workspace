Root Cause Analysis

Why This Gap Exists

The core issue isn't a lack of authority — it's a structural gap between how the Dewberry website is built and what Google's AI systems need to confidently recommend a business.

1. The Wix Platform Ceiling

Dewberry's site is built on Wix, which imposes real limitations on the technical signals that Google's AI relies on most heavily. Wix does not easily support custom JSON-LD schema markup — which is the primary language that tells Google's AI exactly what kind of business you are, what events you run, when they occur, and what the experience involves. Without schema, Google has to guess. Guessing takes time. Time costs rankings.

2. No Event Schema for Seasonal Events

Fall Festival, BOOriffic Nights, A Berry Merry Christmas, and Spring Fest are each massive, distinct events that families are specifically searching for. None of them have Event schema markup that would allow Google's AI to surface them as structured, named events with dates, prices, and descriptions. This is one of the most direct paths to AI Overview inclusion for seasonal businesses — and it's completely unused.

3. Page Content Written for Humans, Not AI

The Dewberry website is visually attractive and emotionally engaging — which is exactly right for a family farm experience brand. But AI systems don't experience emotion; they extract structured information. When Google's AI crawls the Fall Festival page, it encounters a slideshow, some image-heavy sections, and marketing copy that describes the experience without clearly declaring the entity information (exact location, exact services, exact service area, exact dates) in a format AI can parse reliably.

4. Service Area Signals Are Underbuilt

Dewberry draws visitors from across the greater Houston metro — Katy, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, Cypress, Pearland, Missouri City, and beyond. But the website primarily optimizes around "Brookshire, TX" and "near Houston." Competitors that build out explicit service area content for surrounding communities capture those searches more effectively. A family in Katy searching "pumpkin patch near Katy" should find Dewberry immediately — and consistently.

5. The Spring Fest Visibility Problem

Fall Festival and Christmas events get the most marketing attention — understandably. But Spring Fest (March–April) is an increasingly important event that is almost invisible in AI search. Searches for "Easter activities near Houston," "spring farm near Houston," and "Easter bunny Houston" represent real, high-intent seasonal traffic that Dewberry could own with the right structure in place.

Opportunities for Improvement

AI-Optimized Seasonal Landing Pages

Build standalone, AI-native landing pages for Fall Festival, BOOriffic Nights, A Berry Merry Christmas, and Spring Fest — structured to win AI Overview placement for each event's primary search queries. These work alongside the existing Wix site without requiring any changes to it.

🔴 High Priority

Event Schema Implementation

Add JSON-LD Event schema for every seasonal event — dates, ticket prices, descriptions, location. This is the single most direct technical signal that triggers AI Overview inclusions for event-based businesses. Currently completely missing from the Dewberry web presence.

🔴 High Priority

Google Business Profile Seasonal Optimization

Proactively update GBP for each season — photos, event posts, hours, Q&A optimized for AI search intent. GBP is one of Google's primary signals for AI Overview recommendations for local businesses. Current GBP activity is inconsistent between seasons.

🟡 Medium Priority

Service Area Content — Surrounding Communities

Build location-specific content targeting Katy, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, Cypress, Pearland, and Missouri City. Each community represents families actively searching for nearby activities. Dewberry is ideally positioned for all of them but invisible in those searches.

🟡 Medium Priority

Entity Signal Alignment Across Platforms

Audit and align NAP data (Name, Address, Phone) across every platform Google checks — Yelp, TripAdvisor, Facebook, Google, Apple Maps, Bing, and data aggregators. Minor inconsistencies currently exist and reduce entity confidence in Google's systems.

Review Velocity Strategy for Fall 2026

Implement a systematic review generation strategy now — starting with the current Spring Fest season — to build fresh review momentum before the high-stakes fall season. Target 50+ new Google reviews before September with specific, AI-readable language about the fall experience.

🟢 Quick Win

Spring Fest AI Visibility Push

Spring Fest (currently running through April 26) is an immediate opportunity to start building AI visibility momentum. Optimizing for "Easter activities near Houston" and "spring farm near Houston" now creates early signals that compound through summer and into fall.

TikTok & Paid Social Alignment with AI Content

The TikTok and Google Ads work currently driving traffic doesn't have AI-optimized destination pages to land on. Aligning paid social with AI-native landing pages means every dollar of ad spend lands on a page that both converts visitors AND builds AI visibility signals simultaneously.
